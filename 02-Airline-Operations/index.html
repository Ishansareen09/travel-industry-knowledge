TOPIC 01

Airport Processes

Airport processes cover the passenger journey from arrival at the airport through check-in, security, boarding, flight departure and arrival at the destination.

Typical passenger journey

  1. Airport arrival and terminal entry.
  2. Check-in and travel document verification.
  3. Baggage acceptance and tagging.
  4. Security screening.
  5. Immigration formalities where applicable.
  6. Boarding gate verification and aircraft boarding.
  7. Arrival, baggage collection and customs where applicable.
Industry insight: Airport processes vary by airport, airline, route and applicable national regulations.

TOPIC 02

Passenger Handling

Passenger handling includes the activities required to verify travel eligibility, accept passengers for travel and provide appropriate assistance at the airport.

Important passenger categories

  • Adults, children and infants.
  • Unaccompanied minors (UMNR), subject to airline policy.
  • Passengers requiring reduced mobility assistance.
  • Passengers requiring medical or other special assistance.
  • Passengers travelling with pets, where permitted.

Important checks

  • Passenger name and booking details.
  • Passport, visa and destination entry requirements.
  • Ticket and baggage conditions.
  • Special service requests and assistance arrangements.
TOPIC 03

Ground Operations

Ground operations coordinate activities required to prepare an aircraft for its next flight while it is parked at an airport.

Aircraft turnaround activities

  • Aircraft arrival and parking coordination.
  • Passenger disembarkation.
  • Baggage and cargo unloading.
  • Cabin cleaning and catering services.
  • Refuelling and other authorised servicing.
  • Aircraft loading and baggage reconciliation.
  • Passenger boarding and departure preparation.
Operational focus: Effective turnaround coordination helps airlines maintain schedules while following safety and ground handling procedures.
TOPIC 04

Flight Operations

Flight operations involve planning, coordinating and monitoring flights to support safe, compliant and efficient airline services.

Core responsibilities

  • Flight planning and route considerations.
  • Weather and operational information review.
  • Aircraft and crew coordination.
  • Fuel planning and operational requirements.
  • Flight dispatch and operational monitoring.
  • Coordination with air traffic services and airport teams.
Important: Flight dispatch and operational control responsibilities depend on the airline's approved procedures and applicable aviation regulations.
TOPIC 05

Flight Disruption Management

Irregular Operations (IROPS) occur when flights are affected by events that disrupt normal schedules or services.

Common causes

  • Adverse weather conditions.
  • Technical or aircraft maintenance issues.
  • Air traffic restrictions.
  • Airport congestion.
  • Operational or crew availability issues.

Typical response activities

  1. Identify and assess the disruption.
  2. Evaluate available operational alternatives.
  3. Coordinate with relevant operational teams.
  4. Arrange rebooking or other applicable passenger support.
  5. Communicate confirmed information to passengers.
  6. Monitor recovery and service restoration.
TOPIC 06

Aviation Safety & Compliance

Safety and compliance are fundamental to airline operations. Airlines and airport service providers follow applicable regulations, approved procedures and safety management systems.

Important areas

  • Safety Management Systems (SMS).
  • Ground handling safety procedures.
  • Aircraft security and access controls.
  • Dangerous goods awareness and handling requirements.
  • Emergency response and incident reporting.
  • Operational audits and regulatory compliance.
Safety principle: Operational targets must never override safety requirements or approved procedures.

Important Airline Operations KPIs

Airline performance is monitored through operational, punctuality, safety and customer service indicators.

OTP On-Time Performance
Turnaround Time Aircraft ground time between flights
Completion Factor Flights operated versus scheduled
Baggage Performance Baggage handling and mishandling indicators
Load Factor Passenger occupancy relative to available seats
Customer Satisfaction Passenger experience measures
Management tip: Always check the airline's KPI definitions before comparing results. Measurement methods and reporting windows can differ.
